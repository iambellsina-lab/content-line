# Start here: the brand intake

**Do this before you make anything.** Fifteen minutes, once. Without it every post Claude makes will
be competent and generic, which is worse than nothing because it looks like effort.

Fill this in, save it, and tell Claude: *"Read BRAND-INTAKE.md. That is my brand."*

---

## Why this is short

Most brand questionnaires ask forty questions and produce forty answers about who someone wishes they
were. **Three kinds of thing exist here, and only one of them is a question.**

| | How it gets answered |
|---|---|
| **What only you know** | You answer it. Six questions below |
| **What can be measured** | Do not answer. Point Claude at what you already saved and it reads the pattern |
| **What has to be tested** | Do not answer. Guessing it wastes a month. Your own posts settle it |

This was learned the hard way: asked to name a voice, a person describes their aspiration. Shown their
own saved images, the real thing is obvious in ten minutes.

---

## Part 1: the six questions only you can answer

**1. What does the business actually sell, and to whom?**
One sentence each. Not the mission. The transaction.
> We sell ___________ to ___________ , who currently ___________ .

**2. What happens after someone likes a post?**
The next step, concretely. A link, a form, a DM, a call, nothing yet. **If the answer is nothing, say
nothing.** A content system pointing at a dead end is the most expensive mistake available.
> ___________

**3. Who do you want to sit beside?**
Five to fifteen accounts, by handle. Not aspiration, adjacency: people your buyer already follows.
Include small accounts whose posts travel far past their following, they teach the most.
> ___________

**4. What will you never do?**
The real limits. No face on camera, no synthetic voice, no swearing, no politics, no other people's
children, no before-and-after claims, no income screenshots. Anything that would make you wince.
> ___________

**5. What do you know that most people in your niche get wrong?**
This is the only question about content, and it is the one that produces everything else. A real
disagreement is the difference between a voice and a feed.
> ___________

**6. How many hours a week, honestly?**
Not the hopeful number. The one that survives a bad week. Everything gets sized to it.
> ___________

## Part 2: what to point at instead of describing

Do not write adjectives here. Give Claude the place and let it measure.

| What | Where yours lives | What gets taken from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual direction** | A Pinterest board, a saved folder, a moodboard, your camera roll | Type, colour, contrast, whether text sits over pictures, how crowded a frame gets |
| **Your voice** | Anything you already wrote: emails, captions, a newsletter, voice notes | Sentence length, how you open, what you never say, whether you swear |
| **Existing design** | Canva, a logo file, a website | Exact fonts and hex values, so nothing is invented |
| **What already worked** | Your best performing posts, if any | The shape that your audience responded to |

> Boards, folders or links: ___________

**One warning from doing this for real.** A person's boards often split in two. The ones where they
save a MESSAGE and the ones where they save BEAUTY can disagree completely on colour and mood. When
they do, **the beauty boards are the brand.** The message boards show what they admire. The others
show what they actually choose.

---

## Part 3: what not to decide yet

Leave these blank on purpose. Deciding them now means guessing, and each one is cheap to learn and
expensive to assume.

- **How often to post.** Falls out of question 6 and what production actually costs.
- **Which platform matters most.** Falls out of where the buyer in question 1 already is.
- **Format.** Carousels, reels, stills. Research says what to start with, your numbers say what to keep.
- **The hook that works.** This one is genuinely unknowable in advance. It takes about twelve posts.

---

## What happens next

1. Claude reads this file and whatever you pointed it at.
2. It writes a **brand file** (`specs/yourbrand.json`): font, colours, layout, in numbers.
3. It renders one test so you can look at it before anything real is made.
4. **You correct it.** Expect to, twice. Correcting something wrong is faster than describing something
   right, and the corrections are the actual brief.
5. Only then does it make posts.

---

## The two rules that come out of this

**A line has to make a real claim about a real thing, and it is allowed to be funny.** Abstract nouns
are the tell. "Your softness is not a liability" names nothing and happens nowhere. "Nobody writes
poems about the one who remembers to feed the cat" names a cat, a poem and an unglamorous act. The
second one is the brand.

**Borrow the shape, never the claims.** Copying how a winning post is built is craft. Copying what it
says is theft, and their claims are theirs to defend, not yours.
