# What actually produces money, as opposed to attention

**This document exists because a client asked the right question and the answer inverted the system.**
His words: *"There's a bunch of chiropractors who post stuff and they rip people's necks off and they
get a lot of attention. But it doesn't mean anything because their practices suck. I need the social
media to produce money."*

He was right. Researched 2026-10-02. Where a figure is from a named study it is cited. Where the
evidence is thin, this file says so.

---

## 1. The objection is not an instinct. It is a measured result

**Dai, Kim and Luca, "Which Firms Gain from Digital Advertising? Evidence from a Field Experiment",
NBER, February 2023.** A randomised field experiment with Yelp across **18,294 restaurants**, with
**7,209** given free advertising for three months.

The abstract, verified at source: *"a 7-19% increase in a wide range of **purchase intention**
outcomes"* and *"a 5% increase in customer reviews."*

**Read what that abstract does not say.** It reports purchase INTENTION. A randomised experiment of
that size that had found a transaction effect would lead with it.

The paper body, as reported by research rather than read here directly, adds the part that is the
client's complaint exactly: the attention the ads bought **converted about 46% worse** than attention
arriving organically, pulling overall view-to-lead conversion **down 12%**, with effects on actual
reservations and orders statistically insignificant and gains dissipating immediately when spend stops.

**So attention and money diverge, and the divergence has been measured with state tax records in the
appendix.**

---

## 2. Why the neck-crack video fails, and the real reason is more useful

Not "wrong audience". The real mechanism:

> **For a local service, the viral artifact and the sellable artifact are different objects.**

Virality pays when the thing people saw **is** the thing they can buy in one step. It does not pay when
the thing they saw is a spectacle and the purchase requires an appointment, a drive, insurance and a
treatment plan. **The neck crack is the photo op, not the product.**

Documented both directions in named businesses (Food Republic, 25 Oct 2023, trade journalism, unaudited):

- **Converted:** Lafayette Grand Café, Manhattan. A viral croissant took production from 12 to 600+ a
  day. The viral object was the purchasable object, available immediately, zero booking friction.
- **Did not:** Folderol, Paris. Crowds came to photograph the storefront rather than buy. Regulars
  stopped coming. The owners banned posing outside and hired a bouncer.

**A chiropractic adjustment video is the Folderol case, structurally.**

## 3. The thing with the largest measured effect is not content

| Study | Scale | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| HBR 2011, Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington | 1.25M leads | Contact within an hour, **~7x** more likely to qualify than an hour later. **Over 60x** versus 24 hours |
| Oldroyd / Lead Response Management, 2007 | 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ calls | Contact odds **100x** greater at 5 minutes than 30 |
| XANT, 2021 | 5.7M leads, 400+ companies | Conversion **~8x** higher inside 5 minutes |

Average response time among companies that responded at all: **42 hours.**

**No content study in local services produces effect sizes in that range.** Content decides whether a
hand goes up. **Response decides whether it becomes money, at seven to a hundred times.**

*Sceptic's note, because the inflated versions circulate: "78% of customers buy from whoever responds
first" has no traceable primary source. The widely quoted "47 hour average" is a miscitation of HBR's
42.*

## 4. For a local service, social is a validation checkpoint, not a discovery channel

**BMC Oral Health, 2023, Spain, n=503**, people who had changed dental practice:

| Consulted when choosing | |
|---|---|
| Website | **84.7%** |
| Instagram | 17.8% |
| Facebook | 13.6% |
| YouTube | 0.8% |

Only **23.5%** consulted social media before switching. **But of those, 86.4% said it influenced their
decision.** And when ranking what mattered in choosing a practice, **social media presence ranked
lowest.**

**Few people look. Almost everyone who looks is moved.**

That is a low-traffic, high-influence conversion asset, and it means **reach is the denominator of the
wrong fraction.**

## 5. The only local channel with causal revenue evidence is reputation

**Michael Luca, Harvard Business School WP 12-016, "Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of
Yelp.com."** Yelp ratings matched to Washington State Department of Revenue **actual sales data**,
identified off Yelp's star-rounding thresholds so underlying quality is held constant.

> **A one-star increase in displayed rating produces a 5 to 9% revenue increase**, and only for
> independent businesses, not chains.

**That is the highest-quality evidence in the local-business literature and it is about stars, not
posts.** It is not an argument against content. It is an argument about sequencing and about what to
promise.

## 6. Copying a trend is punished. Copying a FORM is not the same thing

**Sprout Social Index 2025**, n=4,044 consumers following 5+ brands:

- **33%** think brands jumping on viral trends is "embarrassing"
- **~50%** say original content is what distinguishes their favourite brands
- **73%** say they will switch to a competitor if a brand does not respond on social

*Caveat: Sprout sells social software and the sample skews engaged. But the trend-chasing finding cuts
against the vendor's own commercial interest, which makes it more credible rather than less. And that
73% lands on the same point as section 3: responsiveness, not posting.*

**Correction, 2026-10-02, and the heading above used to overstate this.** It read "Study what is winning
and copy it is contradicted by consumer data", which is not what Sprout measured. **Sprout measured
brands copying SUBSTANCE**, a trend, a sound, somebody else's joke. **CE-1 copies FORM and explicitly
forbids copying substance:** step 1 is "the substance comes from what you already own" and step 3 is
"borrow the format, never the claims". A post whose words come from her own library, in a shape that
works on the platform, is **original content by Sprout's own definition** and sits on the right side of
that 50%.

So section 6 and CE-1 are measuring different things and the contradiction was mine, not the data's.
Found by the Kit session, 2026-10-02, which refused to act on section 10 for exactly this reason.

## 7. Two things this kit got wrong, corrected

**"Link in bio" is not a lazy habit. It is a platform rule.** Organic Instagram feed posts and Reels
**cannot** carry a clickable link; a URL in a caption renders as plain text. The clickable surfaces are
the bio, Story link stickers and DMs. So the fix is not to stop saying it. **The fix is to make the bio
link resolve differently per post**, and to use the surfaces that do carry native per-item links.

**"Instagram strips referrers so social cannot be tracked" is backwards for this channel.** The one
controlled test available (SparkToro with Really Good Data, ~100 panelists, 1,113 visits, 11 platforms,
April 2023) found Instagram public links showed **minimal** misattribution. What recorded as 100%
direct with no referral data was **TikTok, WhatsApp and Slack**.

## 8. The metrics this system should report

**Headline, per client, weekly: booked appointments that SHOWED, with deal value, split by source.**
Not leads. Not bookings. **Shown and valued**, because the whole objection is that attention produces
no-shows, so the metric must have show-up inside it.

**Operating number, controlled daily: median and 90th-percentile minutes to first human reply**, from
contact creation to first outbound human touch. It is free to improve and almost no local competitor
collects it. **Plan against the 7x, not the 100x.** The 7x rests on 1.25 million leads across 2,241
companies, which is the strongest evidence in this file. The 100x rests on roughly 15,000 leads across
six companies, so it points the right way but is too thin to forecast from. **Target the five-minute
band anyway**, because the cost of trying is zero and both studies agree on the direction.

**Content number: profile-visit-to-contact rate.** Denominator, profile visits. Numerator, new contacts
created in the same window. Follows directly from section 4. **A post seen by 400 locals who then visit
the profile beats 40,000 views that produce none.**

---

## 9. The attribution mechanic, in one line

> **Put the post's identity inside the URL.**

Not a cookie, not a referrer, not a pixel. A parameter or path segment travels inside the URL itself, so
it survives referrer stripping, ad blockers, third-party cookie blocking and Apple's tracking rules.
**It is the only part of attribution that is deterministic rather than modelled.** Everything else is
plumbing around that one fact.

## 10. What this means for the kit, stated plainly

**`niche-research` ranks on views, saves and shares. Those are attention metrics.** It will reliably
identify a popular account that sells nothing, which is the exact failure this document is about.

**`social-engine` says "non-follower reach is the number."** For a local service business that rule is
wrong, and section 4 is why.

**The metric in `social-engine` is the piece that can move on evidence alone**, because it is about which
number to watch and touches no recorded decision. **`niche-research` is not**, and the earlier version of
this section was wrong to say both needed rewriting.

**Why.** CE-1, recorded 2026-10-01 in Bella's own words, is "source from your own thing, mimic the format
that is already winning, never invent", and `DECISIONS.md` calls it the product rather than just her
process. Rewriting `niche-research` against section 6 would gut it. Section 6 as originally written
claimed a contradiction the data does not establish, see the correction there.

**Neither skill should be rewritten without Bella.** The question is staged for her at
`Outbox/DECIDE-copy-what-is-winning.md`.

What stands unchanged: the system is good at producing attention and unproven at producing money, and
anyone selling it should say so.
