# What a webinar funnel actually converts at

Five research agents, 2026-10-02, instructed to say "nobody has reliable data" rather than invent a
number. Raw output preserved at `research/webinar-funnel-raw-2026-10-02.txt`. This is the readable
version. Companion to `WHAT-PRODUCES-MONEY.md`.

**The headline: exactly one step of this funnel has real aggregate data behind it. Every number after
"attended" is somebody's anecdote.** Plan accordingly.

---

## The one step that is measured: show rate

Four independent platform datasets, roughly 80,000 webinars and 14 million registrations, converge:

| Source | Sample | Live show rate |
|---|---|---|
| Livestorm 2026 | 33,786 sessions, 3,199 orgs, 7,062,572 registrations | **47.7%** (83 live of 175 registered) |
| Goldcast 2026 | 26,190 webinars, 522 B2B orgs, 6.8M registrants | 40% per-event, **32.7%** registration-weighted |
| Univid 2026 | 2,000+ webinars, 325,000+ attendees, medians winsorized | **49%** live, 57% with replay |
| GoTo | 250,000 webinars, 17,000+ businesses | **44%** for marketing webinars |

**Plan against 40%. The honest range is 33 to 50%.** Bigger webinars show up worse, so a small one should
expect the top of the range.

**Do not use ON24's 60%.** Their own dataset says on-demand is half their "attendees", so that figure
counts replay watchers. It is not a live show rate.

Average watch time is 26 minutes of a 68-minute session (Livestorm). Build the offer to land before
minute 26, not at minute 55.

## Replay does not rescue the no-shows

- Univid: 86% of views are live. Replay lifts total attendance from 49% to 57%.
- Goldcast: 89.1% of webinars are posted on demand, and on-demand is still only 15.9% of views. An
  average of 14 on-demand views per event.
- Livestorm: **ungated replays got 368% more views than gated ones**, 14.60 against 3.97.

So roughly 55% of registrants miss it and replay recovers about a sixth of them. **That bucket, not the
offer, is the biggest single leak in the funnel.** Leave the replay ungated. They already gave an email.

## Where the data stops, and what is circulating instead

The only aggregate figure past "attended" is Univid's **22% in-webinar CTA click rate** across 2,000+
webinars, rising to 25% with exactly two calls to action. **That is a click, not a purchase.**

There is no aggregate data on attendee-to-purchase, and none at all split by price point. The table that
circulates everywhere, the one that says 8 to 15% converts at $97 to $497 and 1 to 4% at $2,000+, traces
to a single operator who writes that he has built 114 webinars and cites no study, no sample, no source.
**One person, unauditable.** Those are the numbers most likely to end up in a plan and the least evidenced
numbers in the subject. Do not forecast from them.

## The one finding that changes the build

**Chili Piper 2025, nearly 4 million form submissions:** 66.7% of qualified submissions book a meeting
when the scheduler sits on the form itself, against a roughly 30% industry figure when it does not.
Offering a live-call option as well reaches 69.2%. Also from that sample: 14.1% of form fills are
disqualified, 561,977 of them.

**So the 1:1 gets booked inside the room, on the screen, while they are still watching.** Not by an email
sent the next morning. On the largest dataset available on the question that is roughly double the
booking rate, and it costs nothing to build.

Held-rate after booking: the 20 to 30% no-show figure quoted everywhere could not be traced to any
auditable dataset. Plan on 70 to 80% held and treat it as an assumption to measure.

## Where speed to lead bites in a webinar

Not at registration. A registrant is not sitting by the phone. It bites in exactly two places:

1. **Attended, next step not booked.** The webinar ends with 80 to 100 people at the highest intent they
   will ever have. An email sequence starting the next morning has already crossed the expensive line.
2. **The "missed it" bucket**, which is larger.

The anchor is HBR 2011, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads", 1.25 million leads across 2,241 US
companies: contacting within an hour made a firm nearly **7x** more likely to qualify the lead, and more
than 60x more likely than waiting a day. Plan against the 7x.

## The problem with a weekly cadence, which no benchmark covers

Every dataset above measures one-off, heavily promoted events. Registration is a last-minute act:

- Livestorm: **49.6% of registrations arrive in the final week, 15.3% on the day itself.**
- GoTo: 59% in the final week, 33% on the day.

A weekly webinar compresses the promotion window to a few days. The show *rate* may hold or improve,
since there is less lag between registering and attending. **But throughput is set by registrant volume,
and a weekly cadence cannot build the 175 to 251 registrant averages these reports describe.** Expect a
small fraction of that per session.

This is not a reason to abandon a weekly cadence. It is a reason to expect small rooms early, to judge the
first ten weeks on booked calls rather than attendance, and to keep every registrant in a list that gets
promoted to the next week rather than written off.
